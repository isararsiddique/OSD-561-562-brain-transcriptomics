# K_internal_transport: retrospective plan (15 Oct 2026, written before running)

**Problem.** Neither frozen RR23 direction transferred to RRRM2 or RR10. The paper cannot yet say *why*. Two
explanations predict different things:
1. **Cohort difference.** The RR23 direction is real but does not hold in other missions (sex, strain, launch,
   handling). Held-out RR23 animals, which share every cohort property, should then reproduce the direction
   clearly. External estimates would fall below that internal benchmark.
2. **Estimation noise.** With nine animals per group, the frozen direction is itself too noisy to transfer
   anywhere. Held-out RR23 animals would then also show small, uncertain transport, and the external failure would
   be uninformative about cohort differences.

**Test.** Internal transport benchmark in RR23 (flight vs habitat control, nine vs nine animals).
- *Split-half:* every split of each group into 5 training and 4 held-out animals (C(9,5)² = 15,876 splits). Train
  unit directions on the 5 v 5 animals, then score the 4 v 4 held-out animals with exactly the external score
  (equal-cluster mean of u·(held-out FLT mean − held-out GC mean)).
- *Leave-one-pair-out:* train on 8 v 8 animals and score the one held-out FLT animal minus the one held-out GC
  animal (81 pairs), which is closer to the full 9 v 9 training used externally.
- The scores are computed on the cluster panels measured in each external cohort (702 for RR10, 281 for RRRM2) and
  on all 923 clusters.
- A **label-permuted reference** applies the same split-half procedure after randomly permuting FLT/GC labels within
  RR23 (2,000 permutations × 50 splits), to show what "no signal" looks like.

**Reported.**
- The internal benchmark distribution (median, 2.5–97.5% range across splits).
- Its permuted reference.
- Where the published external estimates (RR10 +0.4358 pp, RRRM2 −0.2770 pp, FLT–GC-trained) fall relative to both.
- The fraction of the internal benchmark retained, as external/internal median (descriptive).

**Limits fixed in advance.**
- Split-half training uses fewer animals than the 9 v 9 external training, so it understates achievable internal
  transport. Leave-one-pair-out reduces that gap.
- Splits reuse animals, so the range is not a confidence interval.
- Held-out animals share collection date with training animals, so the benchmark includes any date effect; this
  makes it an upper reference for transport, not a causal flight effect.
- Retrospective: the external outcomes were known when this was planned.

## Amendment 1 (15 Oct 2026, after independent review, before running)
Reviewer concern M3: the benchmark cannot separate a flight direction from an RR23 date/batch axis. Two additions:
1. **Same-date control.** Run the identical split-half benchmark on the habitat-vs-vivarium direction. These groups
   share a collection date, and the split is 5 GC + 4 VIV for training, 4 + 4 held out (C(9,5)·C(8,4) = 8,820
   splits). It is scored on the same panels. If this no-flight axis reproduces internally at a magnitude similar to
   flight–habitat, internal reproducibility is a generic property of within-cohort group differences and says nothing
   specific about flight.
2. **Matched references.** Report point-to-point ratios (external estimate / internal median), not
   upper-limit / median. Compare the observed internal median against the pooled per-split permuted scores
   (2,000 × 50), and also against the per-permutation medians.
