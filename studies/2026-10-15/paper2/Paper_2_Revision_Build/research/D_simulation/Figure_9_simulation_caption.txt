Simulation: a discrete p-value floor governs BY discovery counts in small exact-permutation designs. a) Minimum number of floor-level tied tests k* required for any Benjamini-Yekutieli rejection at 0.05, as a function of balanced design size and family size m, computed exactly from p_min = 2/C(2n,n) and the harmonic factor H_m. Circles mark attainable values; crosses mark k* > m, where BY can reject nothing whatever the effects are. The star is this study's cell (m = 2,769, nine versus nine, k* = 20). b) BY discovery counts over 500 replicates per condition, each point one evaluated 2,769-test family; black bars are medians and the grey band spans the unreachable counts 1-19. The generating effects are identical across full depth and both complementary halves within each replicate. Grey numbers give the percentage of replicates returning zero discoveries. c) The BY adjusted value of a block of k tests tied at the exact floor, q(k) = p_min m H_m / k, crossing 0.05 between k = 19 and k = 20; step curves in the lower third show the observed floor-tie distributions at S = 80 for full depth and half A, on an arbitrary density scale. d) Observed floor ties against discovery count at S = 80, jittered; the step is reject k if k >= k*, else reject nothing. Points above the step are families in which a second discrete level also cleared BY. e) Cosine between the native effect vectors of the two complementary halves, as empirical cumulative distributions for true-effect clusters and null clusters. f) Discovery counts of the two complementary halves of the same reads at S = 80, jittered; dotted lines mark the k* boundary in each half. Halves partition the same observed reads from the same animals and share the biological draw, so they are dependent repeated instances rather than independent replications. The simulation uses a parametric Dirichlet-multinomial count model with one contrast and homogeneous effect magnitudes; it supports the correction-boundary mechanism and no biological claim.
