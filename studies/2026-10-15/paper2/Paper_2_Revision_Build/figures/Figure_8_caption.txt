Figure 8. Identification limits and measured-QC sensitivity in RR23 cerebellar junction profiles. a, The 26-animal design has identical flight and early-collection indicators and rank three for four columns; no regression can separate their coefficients. b, Primary 923-cluster Hellinger-space sensitivity: the lower bound on a remaining contrast under an assumed additive bias norm no greater than λ times the GC–VIV contrast. Solid curves use observed centroids; dotted curves allow a joint 95% bootstrap error radius across the three contrasts. The same-date GC–VIV magnitude is a hypothetical scale benchmark, not a date-effect estimate. Tipping values label the plug-in bound. c, Contrast norm ratios after simultaneous adjustment for RNA integrity number (RIN) and log2 summed library read depth, relative to the group-only model, for the 923-cluster multiway frame and 5,249 binary choices. Bars are approximate 95% percentile intervals from 10,000 joint within-arm animal bootstrap refits; they quantify measured-QC sensitivity, not causal identification. d, Multiway squared centroid distances and the inner product of FLT–VIV with GC–VIV before and after distinct-animal U-statistic correction for sampling noise. Corrected moment bars are approximate basic bootstrap intervals using analytically centered U-statistic errors; negative lower limits are retained. No inference is made from corrected cosine ratios, because most corrected bootstrap contrast Gram matrices are inadmissible. Animals, rather than reads or overlapping clusters, are the resampling unit. All analyses are retrospective and conditional on the frozen measurement frame.
