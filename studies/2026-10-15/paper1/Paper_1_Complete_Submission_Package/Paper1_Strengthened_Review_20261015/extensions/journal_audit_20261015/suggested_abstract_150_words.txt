Collection conditions can overlap biological responses in brain transcriptomics. We reanalysed seven NASA deposits comprising 129 libraries and 83 animals using animal-restricted inference, paired regional tests and count models. Unflown collection cohorts differed in rapid-response expression (mean log2 effect 0.889, q=0.019). Across 362 overlapping ground holdouts with training-only preprocessing, collection-score discrimination was perfect in both core regions, yet original loadings worsened outside-sensor prediction by 9.1% in cerebellum and 3.5% in RR-23. Ground-derived subtraction was an oblique projection with spectral gain 8.13–15.31. A closest-loading constraint bounded amplification while retaining score removal. Across 2,600 independent simulations, bounding moderated instability under changed coupling but could leave substantial nuisance. No paired panel or cross-cohort gene test survived its respective correction. These results distinguish repeatable collection signatures from transferable correction and support reporting stability and uncertainty before interpreting removed expression as recovered biology.
