# Novelty and primary source assessment

The defensible contribution is a spaceflight-specific empirical diagnosis and mathematically constrained correction sensitivity, not a claim to have invented oblique projection or discovered a universal inflammatory pathway. The new real-data experiment separates repeatability of a collection-associated sensor from transfer of a genome-wide regression loading. Training-only filtering and normalization give perfect age-stratified discrimination in every balanced core holdout, while score prediction of outside-sensor expression worsens on average in cerebellum and RR-23. The heldouts overlap and use only 30 ground source animals. This is within-resource validation rather than a newly sampled mission or a clinical prediction claim.

The geometric cap is the standard Euclidean projection of a loading's perpendicular component onto a ball under the sensor constraint. Its application provides a verifiable amplification limit and a clear nuisance-removal tradeoff. It does not establish biological recovery or FDR control. The paper retains failure under changed nuisance coupling, overlapping biology and high-gain diffuse nuisance. Independent simulations and complete real-data sensitivities are a methodological contribution; a comprehensive priority claim has not been established.

The closest literature has different inferential targets:

- Afshinnekoo et al., Cell 2020 (10.1016/j.cell.2020.10.050), summarizes fundamental spaceflight biological responses. It supports the mixed-exposure context, not one causal mechanism for every brain transcriptomic contrast.
- Leek et al., Nature Reviews Genetics 2010 (10.1038/nrg2825), and Nygaard et al., Biostatistics 2015 (10.1093/biostatistics/kxv027), describe high-dimensional batch effects and potentially exaggerated downstream confidence. These concerns predate the present study; the manuscript does not claim to originate them.
- Leek and Storey, PLoS Genetics 2007 (10.1371/journal.pgen.0030161), Risso et al., Nature Biotechnology 2014 (10.1038/nbt.2931), and Gagnon-Bartsch and Speed, Biostatistics 2012/online2011 (10.1093/biostatistics/kxr034), give established unwanted-variation methods. The bounded loading is an explicit safeguard/sensitivity and is not shown universally superior to these approaches.
- Marsh et al., Nature Neuroscience 2022 (10.1038/s41593-022-01022-8), shows artifactual/confounding glial signatures from processing. It does not prove that every bulk glial association in spaceflight is artifactual.
- Tyssowski et al., Neuron 2018 (10.1016/j.neuron.2018.04.001), and Tullai et al., Journal of Biological Chemistry 2007 (10.1074/jbc.M702044200), motivate differing response programs. They do not calibrate an RNA-based euthanasia clock in the included mice.
- Overbey et al., Cell Reports Methods 2022 (10.1016/j.crmeth.2022.100325), discusses collection constraints for spaceflight single-cell/spatial assays. Collection labels remain observational rather than randomized nuisance interventions.
- Holley et al., npj Microgravity 2022 (10.1038/s41526-022-00217-4), characterizes mouse brain expression after spaceflight; Mao et al., FASEB Journal 2020 (10.1096/fj.202001754r), measures vascular oxidative injury. Bulk endothelial marker expression here is not the same functional assay.
- Masarapu et al., Nature Communications 2024 (10.1038/s41467-024-48916-8), uses RR-3 BALB/c female brain spatial and single-nucleus multiomics. The publisher full text explicitly compares bulk RNAseq from the same RR-3 mice: 11 overlapping genes, only two with matching direction. This is biological/assay context; it is not independent validation of RRRM-2/RR-10 correction. An earlier tentative same-mission attribution was corrected by reading the primary paper.
- Roy et al., Cells 2025 (10.3390/cells14060417), reports **decreased** brain inflammasome activation after RR-1 exposure and no significant flight–ground differences in its assayed cytokines. Its title alone must not be cited as increased neuroinflammation.
- Grandke et al., Nature Communications 2026 (10.1038/s41467-026-68737-1), studies 686 small-RNA samples across 13 organs and two ages, with predominantly age-independent responses. It is current relevant biological context and does not test this bulk-mRNA ground-loading transfer safeguard.

All final references are checked against exact DOI metadata in inputs/reference_metadata and results/manuscript_sources.json. Cached publisher/full-text material used for interpretation is in this extension's cache and provenance files. This search supports a clearly differentiated methodological application; it does not demonstrate exhaustive absence of all prior similar algorithms. Accordingly, the manuscript avoids first-ever, universal-discovery and validated-inflammation claims.

The main biological candidate remains an observational response-panel association. Any circadian flight-by-collection interaction must retain its full-family probability, Monte Carlo uncertainty, count-model comparison, and age-support limitations. Hemoglobin-dominated gene interactions require composition-aware interpretation. Cross-cohort reproducibility yielded no corrected genes, so a ranked nominal list is not a new validated signature.
